This slice integrates Grok's executable Residual Commitment kernel into the Rust research layer while preserving the current architectural boundary.

- Candidate A three-variable atom remains authoritative

- explicit, bounded one-pair co-activation pressure test
- reconstruction of the residual field after simulated process death
- developmental divergence tests for different raw streams

## What is deliberately **not** claimed

### Novel-locus topology is not solved

The current allocator gives repeatable identity for identical opaque bytes. It does **not** provide semantic locality. A mixer/hash spreads input bits, so nearby addresses are not evidence of related learned meaning.

### Co-activation is not yet a reconstructable relation

The derived pair fingerprint is not reversible to its parents. The higher-order residual itself survives field reconstruction, but relation endpoints cannot yet be reconstructed from that atom alone. Therefore this is a pressure-test mechanism, not a settled relation representation.

### Co-activation is bounded on purpose

`observe()` accepts at most one prior active locus. It does not create all-pairs higher-order residuals. Unbounded simultaneously viable "good" or "bad" paths are treated as a future runtime conflict/crisis condition rather than permission for combinatorial ground-state growth.

### Exact cancellation stays pruned

If signed evidence brings a commitment to the prune threshold, the locus disappears. The field does not immediately re-birth the incoming force after cancellation.

### Grounding is still unproven

Stable residual structure, divergence, and process-death reconstruction are not sufficient evidence of cognitive grounding. The next benchmark should test whether learned residual structure improves prediction of **held-out external observations** versus raw recency/frequency and feature-similarity controls.
